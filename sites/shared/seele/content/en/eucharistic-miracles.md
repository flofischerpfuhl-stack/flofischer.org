# Eucharistic Miracles

## Introduction

- Based on the PDF originally prepared to persuade Oli.

### Definition of the Eucharist

- _Eucharist_ comes from the Greek _eucharistia_, “thanksgiving.” It is the Catholic sacrament commemorating the Last Supper.
- The Eucharist is the central part of the Mass; the whole Mass may also be called a Eucharistic celebration.
- A _Eucharistic miracle_ may also be called a _miracle of the host_.
- A host is the bread used for the Eucharist.
- At its consecration the host is changed substantially into Jesus Christ: transubstantiation.
- The accidental or sensible properties remain.
- The substance—what the host is—truly changes.

### Definition of a Miracle

> A miracle is an extraordinary sensible effect brought about by God which exceeds the power and order of created nature.

This definition contains five conditions:

1. God alone is its cause.
2. It lies beyond the power of creatures.
3. It lies beyond the created order. The creation of the world or of souls is therefore not a miracle in this strict sense.
4. It is extraordinary.
5. It is perceptible.

### Eucharistic Miracles

- In a broad sense, the term covers every miracle connected with the Eucharist.
- Across the centuries, the Church has recognized approximately 140 such cases.
- The usual process is:
    1. an alleged miracle is reported to the local bishop;
    2. he investigates it or appoints a commission of experts;
    3. the commission prepares a report;
    4. the bishop decides.
- In the narrower sense discussed here, even the accidents of a consecrated host change into flesh and blood.
- Other reported Eucharistic miracles are different—for example, a stolen host becoming heavier the farther it is carried from the church.
- This lecture concentrates on five cases examined with modern medical or scientific methods.

## Lanciano

- Lanciano lies in the province of Chieti in eastern Italy, near the Adriatic coast.
- The traditional account concerns an unknown monk in the eighth century.
- He was said to be “very learned in the natural sciences but ignorant concerning God.”
- He doubted the Real Presence.
- As he spoke the words of consecration, the host is said to have changed into flesh and blood.
- The material is said to have been preserved to this day—which would itself be remarkable.
- Initial examinations took place in 1574, 1770, and 1886.
- In 1970 it was examined by Dr. Odoardo Linoli, chief physician in Arezzo.
- He published his examination in a medical journal.
- The material was re-examined in 1981 and his results were confirmed.
- Those results were again published, this time in _L’Osservatore Romano_.
- Reported results:
    1. the examined substances were real human flesh and blood;
    2. the tissue came from the myocardium—that is, heart tissue;
    3. both flesh and blood were type AB.
- A frequently alleged United Nations report is false.

## Buenos Aires

- Date of the event: 15 August 1996.
- Location: Buenos Aires, Argentina.
- A woman told the parish priest that she had found a host on a candleholder.
- Father Alejandro Pezet did not consume it because it was dirty.
- The customary procedure is to place such a host in water and allow it to dissolve.
- He placed it in water, and over the following days it changed and slowly became red.
- The professional photographer Marcello Antonini documented the development.
- After one month, the substance was placed in a container of distilled water and stored for three years.
- Distilled water is not a favorable medium for preserving biological material.
- In 1999, the bishop of Buenos Aires ordered an investigation. That bishop was Jorge Bergoglio.
- The examination was extensively documented by a camera team:
    - documentary filmmaker Ron Tesoriero;
    - investigative journalist Mike Willesee, who studied many alleged medical miracles after converting to Christianity.
- Dr. Ricardo Castañón Gómez, a clinical psychologist, directed the investigation.
- Dr. Vanora M. Kean carried out a medical examination of the substance.
- Reported results:
    1. human blood;
    2. blood type AB;
    3. human genetic material;
    4. a DNA profile that could not be identified more precisely.
- Later that year, forensic pathologist Dr. Robert Lawrence examined it:
    - the white blood cells were in very good condition;
    - they appeared to have been alive when the sample was taken;
    - they should have broken down after contact with water.
- Lawrence was an atheist.
- In 2001, Professor Linoli—the investigator of Lanciano—examined the sample and judged it very probably to be heart tissue.
- In 2002, Professor John Walker of the University of Sydney found intact muscle cells and white blood cells.
- In 2005, it was shown to Dr. Frederick Zugibe:
    - Zugibe was a forensic pathologist and leading expert in forensic cardiology;
    - he was not told what the sample was;
    - he confirmed that it was heart tissue;
    - he also concluded that the patient had been under severe stress.

Zugibe is quoted as saying:

> Because your patient has some thrombi which at certain moments impede breathing and oxygen supply, they would exhaust the patient and cause suffering, since every breath must have been painful. He probably received a blow to the chest. Moreover, the heart was highly active at the time the sample was brought to me. The white blood cells were intact, and these are transported only by blood. If white blood cells are present, it means that the heart was beating when the sample was taken.

When told the sample’s claimed origin, he reportedly answered:

> I do not believe it. Doctor, when you brought me this sample, this heart was alive!

- He doubted that the tissue had been stored in water, because autolysis should have dissolved it.
- An independent examination found no preserving chemicals.
- In summary, the reported findings were:
    1. human flesh and blood;
    2. blood type AB;
    3. heart tissue;
    4. an inconclusive DNA test;
    5. unexplained preservation of the tissue and white blood cells.

## Tixtla

- Date: 6 October 2006.
- Place: Tixtla in southwestern Mexico.

One account summarizes the event as follows:

> On 21 October 2006, during a Eucharistic celebration in Tixtla in the Diocese of Chilpancingo–Chilapa, a reddish substance was observed issuing from a consecrated host. The local bishop, the Most Reverend Alejo Zavala Castro, convened a theological commission of inquiry and in October 2009 asked Dr. Ricardo Castañón Gómez to direct the scientific research program intended to examine the event. The Mexican ecclesiastical authorities approached Dr. Castañón Gómez because they knew that between 1999 and 2006 he had studied two consecrated hosts which had also bled in the parish of St. Mary in Buenos Aires. The Mexican case began in October 2006 when Father Leopoldo Roque, pastor of St. Martin of Tours, invited Father Raymundo Reyna Esteban to lead a spiritual retreat for his parishioners. As Father Leopoldo and another priest distributed Communion, assisted by a religious sister standing to Father Raymundo’s left, she turned toward him holding the pyx with tears in her eyes. This immediately drew the celebrant’s attention: the host she had taken to give Communion to a woman had begun to exude a reddish substance.

- Dr. Gómez published the results of his investigation in a book.
- Reported results:
    1. the red substance was human blood;
    2. the material had been affected by autolysis;
    3. it contained initially unidentified muscle tissue, later identified as heart tissue;
    4. it contained several types of white and red blood cells;
    5. the human DNA could, inexplicably, not be identified more precisely;
    6. the tissue was interwoven with the host, and the blood appeared to issue from inside the host as though through a vein.

## Sokółka

- Date: 12 October 2008.
- Place: Sokółka in eastern Poland.
- A consecrated host fell to the floor during Mass.
- It was placed in water so that it could dissolve.
- Seven days later, a religious sister noticed a light-red mark on it.
- The local archbishop was informed and went to the site with other officials.

> On 29 October, the container holding the host was moved to the Divine Mercy chapel in the rectory and kept in the tabernacle. The next day, on the archbishop’s instructions, the stained host was removed from the water and placed on a small corporal, which was returned to the tabernacle. It was kept this way for three years, until it was solemnly brought into the church on 2 October 2011. During the first year it remained secret while Church authorities considered what to do with a sign they believed required interpretation. By mid-January 2009, the changed fragment had dried naturally and remained like a bloodstain or clot; its appearance has not changed since. That month the archbishop requested histopathological studies. On 30 March he established an ecclesiastical commission to investigate the phenomenon.

- Polish medical researchers published their scientific findings.
- Reported results:
    1. the substance was heart tissue;
    2. it appeared to have been taken from a living person;
    3. it did not dissolve despite having been placed in water;
    4. the tissue was inseparably interwoven with the host.

## Legnica

- Date: 25 December 2013.
- Place: Legnica in Silesia, southwestern Poland.
- Father Andrzej Ziombra dropped a host on the floor.
- It was placed in water so that it could dissolve.
- Eleven days later, he noticed that roughly one fifth of the host had become red.
- Bishop Stefan Cichy ordered him to observe it for another two weeks.
- During that period the entire remainder of the host dissolved; only the red portion remained.
- In January 2014, physicians in the department of forensic medicine at Wrocław Medical University began a scientific examination.
- It was later also examined by physicians in the department of forensic medicine at the Pomeranian Medical University in Szczecin.
- Reported results:
    1. human heart tissue;
    2. inconclusive DNA tests;
    3. indications that the heart had been under stress.

## Summary

The five cases share the following reported features:

- in each case, a host changed into human flesh and, in some cases, blood;
- each was examined by independent experts using modern scientific or medical methods;
- both Christian and atheist investigators participated;
- several cases involved tissue resistant to normal autolysis;
- heart tissue under stress was identified more than once;
- white blood cells were reported in an unusually good state of preservation.

## Possible Explanations

Natural explanations should be considered first because natural events are ordinarily more common and therefore have a higher prior probability.

Criteria for comparing competing hypotheses include:^[A. Mackonis, “Inference to the Best Explanation, Coherence and Other Explanatory Virtues,” _Synthese_ 190, no. 6 (2011): 975–995, [doi:10.1007/s11229-011-0054-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-011-0054-y).]

1. **Coherence:** Does the hypothesis conflict with our background knowledge?
2. **Breadth:** How many facts does it explain? Relativity, for example, has greater breadth than Newtonian mechanics.
3. **Depth:** An explanation is better when it explains facts which themselves explain further facts; explanatory force is inherited.
4. **Simplicity:** How many additional entities must be postulated to explain the event?

### Natural Hypothesis 1: Bacterium or Fungus

- _Serratia marcescens_ is a bacterium that can occur on bread and make it appear red.
- _Neurospora crassa_ is a mold that can turn bread red.
- _N. crassa_ caused an alleged Eucharistic miracle in Paterson, New Jersey.
- One article claims, without citing a source, that _S. marcescens_ was found on the host in Sokółka.
- Even if it was present there, it would not explain every reported feature.
- The hypothesis has fatally limited explanatory breadth.

### Natural Hypothesis 2: Conspiracy or Fraud

Motives for fraud can be imagined:

- personal prestige: “I am so important that God gives me a miracle”;
- tourism;
- even an ostensibly good motive, such as strengthening belief in the Real Presence.

Yet the hypothesis fares poorly on coherence and complexity:

- Were all the experts bribed?
- How was heart-muscle tissue obtained?
- How was the rarest major blood type obtained?
- Why choose such an elaborate method instead of a simpler fraud?

Its explanatory breadth is also weak:

- How was autolysis prevented?
- How were white blood cells preserved?
- How was tissue interwoven with the host?

### Supernatural Hypothesis 1: Demons

On one Protestant reading, the devil uses false miracles to persuade people of the allegedly false Catholic faith. Demons can produce extraordinary signs; compare Exodus 7:8–12:

> The LORD said to Moses and Aaron: When Pharaoh says to you, “Perform a miracle to authenticate yourselves,” then say to Aaron, “Take your staff and throw it down before Pharaoh.” It will become a snake. When Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh, they did as the LORD had commanded. Aaron threw down his staff before Pharaoh and his servants, and it became a snake. Then Pharaoh summoned wise men and sorcerers, and they, the magicians of Egypt, did the same through their magic: each threw down his staff, and the staffs became snakes. But Aaron’s staff swallowed their staffs.

Coherence is the decisive weakness:

- the alleged phenomena are uncharacteristic of demonic action;
- no clear demonic motive explains them without entering a separate theological controversy.

### Supernatural Hypothesis 2: Miracle

Measured against the same criteria:

1. **Coherence:** neutral overall, very strong concerning the absence of ad hoc additions.
2. **Breadth:** very strong.
3. **Depth:** neutral.
4. **Simplicity:** one additional entity—God.

Taken by itself, this is the best explanation considered here. It becomes stronger in conjunction with independent arguments for God and Catholicism.

## Lessons for Us

Recall the criteria for a miracle:

1. God alone is its cause.
2. It lies beyond the power of creatures.
3. It lies beyond the created order.
4. It is extraordinary.
5. It is perceptible.

A miracle is perceptible because God intends to show something.

The contexts of these reports are suggestive:

- **Lanciano:** doubt concerning the Real Presence;
- **Buenos Aires:** a host left on a candleholder;
- **Tixtla:** distribution by an extraordinary minister of Holy Communion;
- **Sokółka and Legnica:** a host dropped on the floor.

Every case therefore involves either explicit doubt about the Real Presence or conduct toward the Eucharist that may reflect such doubt. This invites further questions: Communion on the tongue? Kneeling? Should unconsecrated hands touch Jesus at all?

At minimum, the events are understood as calls to a stronger faith in the Real Presence. I hope this lecture has strengthened yours. During Eucharistic adoration, remember what the doctrine claims: Christ is really present. The reports examined here claim that his blood and the flesh of his heart have repeatedly been found upon consecrated hosts by scientific examination.
