Eucharistic Miracles

An Exploration of Recent Scientific Data and an Argument for Catholicism

What are Eucharistic Miracles?

An “Eucharistic Miracle” in general is, when a consecrated host or wine transforms into real flesh or blood. Other forms of eucharistic miracles, such as when the host is preserved over an unusually long time, or survives being thrown into fire will not be discussed in this paper. The catholic church teaches that by means of the consecratory Eucharistic Prayer the host and wine really turn into the flesh and blood of Jesus Christ. In a quasi-Aristotilian tradition the church teaches, that the accidents are unaffected while the substance truly changes. With Eucharistic Miracles it is different. Here the accidents are transformed too.1 Is should be noted, that the catholic church has no special procedure for the canonization of eucharistic miracles.2 In Catholicism miracles are defined as “an extraordinary sensible effect wrought by God that surpasses the power and order of created nature”3. After investigation, by the nature of the subject scientific investigation, the bishop of the region where it occurred can canonize it.4

Where have Eucharistic Miracles occurred?

Over the last two centuries over one hundred Eucharistic Miracles in the broad sense (every miracle involving the Eucharist) have occurred.5 Of those miracles, five have involved the host turning into real flesh or blood and have been investigated by modern scientific methods. These are the Eucharistic Miracles of Lanciano, Buenos Aires, Tixla, Sokolka and Legnica.6 These are the Miracles this paper will be focused on.

Lanciano

Catholic Tradition tells, that in the 700s an unknown monk lived in a monastery in Lanciano, who was described as “versed in the sciences of the world but ignorant in that of God. Apparently, he had been plagued by doubts about transubstantiation: he agonized over whether the bread and wine changed substantially into the Body and Blood of our Lord at the words of consecration, and whether our Lord was truly present in the Holy Eucharist.”7 When the monk pronounced the words of consecration during Mass, the host miraculously changed into flesh and blood. After investigations in 1574, 1770 and 1886 Pope Paul VI permitted a series of scientific studies in 1970.8 The study was conducted by Dr. Ordoardo Linoli, head of the hospital in Arezzo, and professor of anatomy, histology, chemistry and clinical microscopy, with the assistance of Prof. Ruggero Bertelli University of Siena.9

Dr. Ordoardo Linoli 10

The interesting results were the following:

  1. The “flesh” and “blood” preserved in Lanciano really were flesh and blood and belonged to the human species

  2. The flesh tissue belonged to the myocardium (a heart muscle tissue11)

  3. The blood group was AB and it was the same in both flesh and blood

  4. The possibility of an ancient fake is extremely unlikely (Linoli describes this possibility as unacceptable citing “indisputable objective elements” in the diagnosis of myocardium and reports this assessment as supported by Prof. Bertelli. In the next paragraph he describes the difficulties required to take such a part of heart tissue from a human corps and asserts that only a medical expert could have done that.) ⁶

The article was published in Quaderni Sclavo di Diagnostica Clinica e di Laboratorio12 a journal published in Sienna.13 The journal most certainly is a legitimate medical journal, as many of its articles are cited on “pubmed.com”14, although it appears not to be peer-reviewed. ⁶

The findings were confirmed by a subsequent study in 1981 which was also done by Dr. Linoli and published in “L´Osservatore Romano”.15

There are also reports about an investigation done by a medical commission of United Nations and World Health Organization which supposedly supported the findings of Dr. Linoli.³ Dr. Franco Serafini who published a book on the subject of eucharistic miracles16 reports to have read the report when he was allowed access to the original report by the friars in Lanciano and concluded that it was a forgery. The report was actually about Egyptian mummies and seams to have been labeled with a new cover so it appeared to be about the Miracle in Lanciano. Serafini thinks this was done by someone who unnecessarily wanted to prop up the scientific evidence.17

Buenos Aires

The Eucharistic Miracle of Buenos Aires began on the 15th of August in 1996 when in the parish of Santa Maria y Caballito Almagro a woman came up to the priest, Father Alejandro Pezet, and informed him, that she found a host placed on a candle holder. As it was dusty, Father Pezet did not want to eat it, so it was put into water to dissolve, as is standard procedure. In the coming days it transformed into real flesh and blood. On Monday, the 26 of August parts of the flesh had turned red. This was Photographed by professional Photographer Marcello Antonini.

Host on the 26 of August, Photographed by Marcello Antonini

In the next days most of the white parts of the Host had also turned red. During these days the location of the host was changed to another tabernacle and Father Petez claimes nobody had acces to it.

Host a few days after 26 of August, Photographed by Marcello Antonini, the white parts of the host seem to be interwoven with the flesh

After a month the tissue was transferred into a glass test tube filled with distilled water, where it was undisturbed for three years. In 1999 Archbishop Bergoglio (who would become Pope Francis) opened an investigation. This investigation was probably the best documented, as it was accompanied by cameras for a film by Lawyer and Documentary Film maker Ron Tesoriero and Australian investigative journalist Mike Willesee, who has studied numerous supposed miracles after converting to Christianity.18 The investigation was done by Dr. Ricardo Castañon-Gomez, a clinical Psychologist19. A sample for testing was taken in October of 1999 and was sealed while being filmed. The sample was sent to “Forensic Analytical” in Haywood California on October 21st 1999. The Analysis was done by Dr. Vanora M. Kean, PhD. The analysis found:

  • human blood of the type AB-positive

  • human genetic code

  • DNA wasn´t further identified

In the same year forensic pathologist Dr. Robert Lawrence of San Francisco also did an analysis and found:

  • white blood cells in very good condition (normally good enough condition for conclusive DNA-Test)

  • white blood cells appear to be active and living when collected

Dr. Lawrence also mentioned that white blood cells would have normally dissolved when laced into water just minutes to two hours after being placed there. Dr. Lawrence is non-religious and does not believe in god.20

In 2001 the sample was tested by Dr. Linoli, who did the tests on the miracle of Lanciano, and found, that it was most likely heart tissue.

In 2002 Prof. John Walker from the University of Sydney found intact muscle cells and white blood cells.

In 2005 the sample was presented to New York forensic pathologist Dr. Frederick Zugibe, a leading expert in cardiac pathology, who was not informed what he was looking at. Zugibe proports to have written over one hundred papers on cardiology which appears to be true21. Zugibe found that it was indeed heart tissue. He also concluded, that the person had a heart attack which he survived for a few days or was beaten up around the chest. In an article his explanation of this fact is quoted as follows:

“Because your patient has some thrombus, at certain times preventing breathing, oxygen supply, tiring and making the patient suffer because every breath had to be painful. He probably suffered a blow to the chest. On the other hand, cardiac activity was high at the time you brought me the sample. The white blood cells were found intact and these are only transported by blood. Therefore, if there are white blood cells, it’s because at the time you brought me the sample, it was beating.”

After his test Zugibe was informed of the tissue´s origin. He was really surprised and at first didn´t believed it. He is quoted as saying: “I don’t believe it. [] Doctor, when you brought me that sample, that heart was alive!”

He also commented on the preservation of white blood cells, even though it was placed in water. He said that it couldn´t have been water because during autolysis the body would release enzymes that would destroy the tissue. The fast death of white blood cells correlated to the time after death through autolysis is supported by independent scientific papers.22 23

After that the sample was tested by the University of Queensland who concluded that the liquid was not formaldehyde, as Zugibe suspected, and no conserving chemicals were found. The liquid was “essentially water”. When talking with the analysist, the film maker Ron Tesoriero also mentioned the “white blood cells that you see there”, making a swap of the samples practically impossible, which would mean that the preservation of the white blood cells alone would appear to be a miracle.

In conclusion, this was a well-documented investigation with four major findings:

  1. It was human flesh and blood

  2. The blood had the type AB-positive

  3. The flesh was from the heart

  4. DNA-Test were inconclusive

  5. The preservation of white blood cells in water is normally practically impossible 24 25 26 27

Tixtla

There already is a perfect summary of the events that happened at Tixtla:

“October 21, 2006, the effusion of a reddish substance was noted from a consecrated Host during the Eucharistic Celebration at Tixtla, in the Diocese of Chilpancingo-Chilapa. The Bishop of the place, Most Reverend Alejo Zavala Castro, then convened a Theological Commission of investigation and, in October 2009, he invited Doctor Ricardo Castañón Gómez, to take on the leadership of the program of scientific research whose purpose was in fact that of verifying the said event. The Mexican Ecclesiastical authorities turned to Doctor Castañón Gómez because they were aware that, in the years 1999-2006, the scientist had conducted some studies on two consecrated Hosts that also bled in the Parish of Saint Mary, in Buenos Aires. The Mexican case starts in October 2006, when Father Leopoldo Roque, pastor of the Parish of Saint Martin of Tours, invites Father Raymundo Reyna Esteban to lead a spiritual retreat or his parishioners. As Father Leopoldo and another priest were distributing Communion, assisted by a religious sister who was to the left of Father Raymundo, this latter one turns towards him with the ”pix”containing the Sacred Particles, looking at Father with eyes filled with tears, an incident that immediately attracted the attention of the celebrant: the Host that she had taken to give Communion to a lady parishioner had begun to effuse a reddish substance.”28

The Host from Tixla, Picture from Gomez´ Book as cited

Dr. Ricardo Gómez has published the scientific findings in a book he wrote29 and we find the scientific findings from pages 113 onward:

  1. The red substance is tested positive for human blood

  2. The material is affected by autolysis (this is due to the methods used by the analysis)

  3. The first study suggested a not further identifiable muscle tissue, it was later identified as part of the heart

  4. Different kinds of red and white blood cells were found

red and white blood cells are identified

“we can see acidophilic elements that remind erythrocytes (red blood cells) degenerated, among them we can see other basophilic elements (white blood cells) that seem to have lobed nuclei and others with rounded nuclei”

  1. The DNA was human but could not be further identified. (This was curious because enough material seemed to be there. One Analysist said “However, and surprisingly, the new DNA analysis was again completely 120 negative, it was not possible to obtain for example the PCR amplification, even though the sample to be analyzed was abundant... I have no explanation for this unusual phenomenon.”)

  2. The hypothesis, that the substance was placed on top of the host is extremely unlikely as two different studies have found, that the substance comes out of the host. In a corresponding report by expert, signed by Dr. Eduardo Sánchez, it explicates: “Blood comes out from its interior towards its periphery, that is, the blood comes from the interior to the exterior, coinciding like a large vessel that bleeds when it is lacerated in its anatomical structure”.

"This way, it can be seen that the center of the image, shown on the left, has a semicircular shape (place where the sample was taken under study). On the other hand, the image shown on the right shows a dispersion along its entire structure, with a central area that seems to cross the entire contour or thickness of its structure. In this way, it can be seen that the blood comes from the interior, being able to be absorbed by the thickness of its surface. In other words, it is possible to appreciate that the structure of the host is of the same characteristics for being a single piece, having for all its extension a single infiltration in its center as if it was a glass that bled to the outside. It is for this probability that the blood found in the analyzed sample and of which the necessary images are shown, has a direction from the inside towards the outside taking into consideration the thickness, consistency and macroscopic and microscopic characteristics observed in the present expert study"

  1. A analysis of the blood with even more modern technologies identified fresh blood. This is surprising as the analysis was done in 2010 but the sample is from 2006.

All these findings are, as mentioned, presented in Gómez´ book as cited in citation 26. In this book, the author also lists all the experts involved on page 116.

A documentary done by Mike Willesee who also accompanied the investigation in the miracle of Buenos Aires lead to doubts about the miracle as it claimed to have found female DNA in a sample of the host collected for an analysis for the documentary.30 However as Dr. Franco Serafini explains, mentioning a late autobiography of Mike Willesee, the sample tested was actually from a different miracle in South America, were the DNA could be matched to the owner of a Jesus Statue involved in this different miracle.31

Sokolka

The Eucharistic miracle took place on Sunday, October 12, 2008 when at the parish church of St. Anthony in Sokolka, at 8:30 a.m. a consecrated host fell down on the floor. After that it was put into water to dissolve, as is standard procedure. At the request of the pastor, Fr. Stanislaw Gniedziejko, Sister Julia Dubowska, of the Congregation of the Eucharistic Sisters, who was the parish sacristan poured the water with the host into another container because the host would take time to dissolve. It was then placed into a safe to which only the pastor and the sister had acces. On October 19 the sister noticed, that there was a bright red stain in the middle of the host. The water seemed to be untainted by the color. Metropolitan archbishop of Bialystok, Edward Ozorowski, was immediately informed and he went to the parish with other diocese officials. “On October 29, the container with the host was transferred to the Divine Mercy Chapel in the rectory, and placed in the tabernacle. The next day, by decision of the archbishop, the stained host was taken out of the water and placed on a small corporal, which was then put back in the tabernacle. The host was kept this way for three years, until it was solemnly brought to the church on October 2, 2011. During the first year, it was kept secret. During that time, the Church authorities reflected on what to do, since they were dealing with a sign from God which needed to be interpreted. By mid-January of 2009, the altered fragment of the host had dried out naturally, and remained like a blood stain or clot; since then, its appearance has not changed. That same month, the archbishop requested histopathological studies be done on the host. On March 30, he created an ecclesial commission to study the phenomenon.” 32 33

The host in question34

The scientific findings are summarized in the article “Eucharistic Miracle from a scientific perspective” by Zbigniew Jacyna-Onyszkiewicz, Maria Elżbieta Sobaniec-Łotowska, Stanisław Tadeusz Sulkowski, Andrzej Kakareko and Miroslaw Rucki. This article has good citations and lists all the medical professionals involved in the analysis. A quick search on researchgate.net, where the article is published, reveals that at least two of the authors have published many medical papers unrelated to Christianity or supernatural events. Maria Elzbieta Sobaniec-Lotowska has published over 60 papers35 and Miroslaw Rucki has published over 15036. The article claimes that the analysists wre not informed what they were analyzing. The findings of the analysis´ include:

  1. The substance appears to be heart tissue

  2. The sample appeared to be taken from a living person in agony

  3. The tissue didn´t dissolve even though it was placed in water (“The Host remained in water for a long time and it remained in the corporal for an even longer period of time. The tissue that appeared on the Host would therefore have had to undergo the process of autolysis, namely the process of self-destruction by the action of the intracellular enzymes; in the material analyzed there were not however observed traces of these alterations!”37)

  4. The tissue formed an inseparable structure with the remains of the host (“The way of connecting the muscle tissue with the Host material, according to scientists, excludes the possibility of human intervention to artificially obtain such an effect”)

The church collected these scientific findings together with witness testimony and theological interpretations and published it in 2009, since then “no objections have been raised from the scientific community as to the way the sample was taken, the methodology of the research carried out and the scientific credibility of the results of the expertise”. 38 It is also noteworthy that Professor Sobaniec-Łotowska said about the interconnectedness of the tissue with the host: “Even the scientists of NASA, who have at their disposal the most modern analytical techniques, would not be able to artificially recreate such a thing”39.

The two professors who were responsible for the analysis40

It should be noted that many people doubt the miracle. The reason is probably a polish article which claims, without sources, that on the host the bacterium Serratia marcescens was found. This is a bacterium that can appear on bread and make it look red and is a candidate for false eucharistic miracles like neurospora crassa, which was found as the reason for a supposed eucharistic miracle in Paterson.41 But even the article notes, that “To this day, I do not understand how a skilled histopathologist could confuse cells as characteristic as heart muscle cells with a much smaller bacterial cell”.42 One explanation (if the bacterium was even really found there) would of course be that there was heart tissue *and* the bacterium. But the following should be noted nonetheless: “Only in 2009, since the discovery of the two professors, their home university has distanced itself. It was emphasized that the research had not been officially commissioned, reported to any of the superiors or registered at the university.”43

Legnica

On December 25 2013, during communion, the priest, Andrzej Ziombra, let a host fall to the ground. It was then put into water to dissolve. On the 4th of January the priest noticed that 1/5 of the host had turned red. He then informed Bishop Stefan Cichy, who ordered him to observe the host for the next two weeks. After this time the host had fully dissolved except for the red part.44

The host the red part after it was separated 45

The scientific investigation began on January 26, 2014, when 15 samples were collected by scientists from the Department of Forensic Medicine at the Medical University in Wrocław. The researchers could exclude that the redness was the result of fungi or bacteria. When they did histopathological analyzes, it showed that some fragments resembled heart muscle but they were not able to identify any DNA. For a subsequent analysis with more advanced methods the investigation commission turned to the Department of Forensic Medicine, Medical University of Szczecin, headed by pathologist Prof. Mirosław Parafiniuk. In this research facility corpses from World War II are analyst to identify them, so they are experts in that field. In their statement the Institute concludes: “The histopathological image showed tissue fragments containing fragmented parts of the striated muscle. \[...\] The whole picture \[...\] is most similar to the heart muscle \[...\] with changes that often accompany agony. Genetic studies show that the tissue is human.” 46

So we can summarize the results of both tests as follows:

  1. It was heart tissue

  2. The tissue was human

  3. DNA-Tests were inconclusive

  4. The heart was in a state of agony

Comparing the miracles

Now we want to examine the similarities and differences of the miracles we looked at.

The fact that they all turned into real human flesh (and blood) is not surprising as this was the criterium for looking at them. The fact that the DNA-Tests were inconclusive is only in so far relevant as they did not find different DNA in the different miracles. The same is true for the blood-type. That all of the flesh is heart tissue is rather surprising as it would be unnecessarily difficult to fake because it had no prior theological necessity. One can read into that, but the appearance of any real flesh on a consecrated host would be enough for a eucharistic miracle. That the flesh and Host were not interconnected in some of the miracles speaks not against them, but in the cases where they were it speaks to the unnecessary difficulty one would have to go through to fake it. It should also be noted, that in many cases the heart seems to be in agony.

Real or Fake

If these were really eucharistic miracles it would not speak against the catholic doctrine of transubstantiation as these miracles do not exclude a change in form of the host. The tradition of Lanciano, of a monk doubting the doctrine of the real presence of Jesus in the Eucharist, would show us a perfectly reasonable explanation for these miracles. It would also explain the fact that they seem to have occurred more often in the last decades as believe in the real presence has rapidly declined in the last years.48

If these miracles were fake on the other hand there would have been experts needed to remove heart tissue from people in some cases living people. Also the people would have to have the blood-type AB, which is the rarest blood type.49 And even if this happened there still would be no explanation for the conservation of white blood cells in water in the Buenos Aires case or the way the host is interwoven with the heart tissue in the Sokolka case. The other explanation would be a conspiracy of all involved experts.

It should also be noted that if the flesh was truly of Jesus Christ it wouldn´t be surprising that the heart tissue was in agony like after being beaten on the chest as it happened to Jesus when he was tortured before being crucified. On the other hand, if somebody faked it, it would not be necessary, as in a few miracles it was not detected that the body had been in pain and stress. It would of course also be extremely difficult as one would have to find the right corps or even kill somebody. It´s also interesting that Dr. Zugibe noted that the tissue looked like it was taken from a living person, which would be even more difficult to fake.

Evidence that demands a verdict

This is of course not an air-tide prove of the truth of Christianity, but if you had to simply look at the evidence and come to a verdict as a judge has to, what would you decide? I think if one does not put the prior probability of the existence of god or the truth of Christianity at zero, it is clear that the theistic explanation is far superior to the naturalistic explanation.
